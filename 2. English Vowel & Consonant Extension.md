# **English Vowel & Consonant Extension Set**

### **For British English (RP / Standard Southern British)**

---

# **GENERAL SPEAKER INSTRUCTIONS**

1. **Hold each target sound for 4–5 seconds.**
   Example: "eeeeee—" (hold it steady, don't let it waver)

2. **Take a clear breath between sounds** to reset.

3. **Maintain stable pitch and volume** unless otherwise indicated.

4. **Don't overthink the technical terms** — focus on what your mouth *feels* like, not on getting the anatomy perfect.

5. For diphthongs, move *slowly* between the two positions so the glide is clearly audible.

---

# **1. PRIMARY VOWELS**

---

## **I. Front Vowels**

---

### **/iː/** — "eeeeee—" (as in _see_)

**Say it like:** Smile and say "ee" — stretch the corners of your mouth sideways as if posing for a photo.
**Feel it:** Tongue is pushed forward and high, tip resting behind your lower teeth. Jaw almost shut. There's tension in the tongue.

**Examples:** see, meat, tree, key, tea, beach, least, repeat

---

### **/ɪ/** — "ih—" (as in _bit_)

**Say it like:** Start from your /iː/ position, then completely relax your face — less stretch, less effort.
**Feel it:** Tongue is still near the top but no longer pushed forward. Less tension, less lip spread than /iː/.

> **Note:** This is *not* the same as /iː/. /iː/ is tense and long; /ɪ/ is relaxed and short.

**Examples:** bit, hill, king, swim, list, middle, ship, dinner

---

### **/e/** — "eh—" (as in _get_)

**Say it like:** Say "bed" and hold the vowel. Or think of the letter name "e" but shorter and more open.
**Feel it:** Mouth opens a little more than /ɪ/. Tongue sits mid-height — not high, not low.

**Examples:** bed, tell, help, dress, fell, many, ready, bread

---

### **/æ/** — "aa—" (as in _cat_)

**Say it like:** Open your jaw wide and say "ah", then push the sound forward toward your teeth. Think of someone saying "that's bad!" in surprise.
**Feel it:** Jaw drops, tongue is flat and near the front. Lips slightly spread but not rounded.

**Examples:** cat, map, back, hand, trap, jacket, candle, athlete

---

## **II. Central Vowels**

---

### **/ɜː/** — "er—" (as in _burn_)

**Say it like:** Make the thinking hesitation sound "errr…" — like you're searching for a word. No lip rounding at all.
**Feel it:** Tongue sits in the middle of your mouth, neither forward nor back. This is the most neutral, tension-free vowel.

**Examples:** bird, her, work, learn, nurse, word, journey, service

---

### **/ʌ/** — "uh—" (as in _cup_)

**Say it like:** A short, surprised "uh!" — like you've just been lightly poked. Let the jaw drop slightly.
**Feel it:** Tongue is low and central, relaxed. Lips are open but not rounded or spread.

**Examples:** sun, love, tough, mud, strut, jump, cover, country

---

### **/ə/** — (schwa) — "uh—" (very brief, unstressed)

**Say it like:** This is the laziest sound in English. It's the "uh" in "sofa" or "about" — mumbled and short. Don't try to make it strong or clear.
**Feel it:** Everything is relaxed. No jaw effort, no lip shape, tongue just floating in the middle.

> **Note:** You can't really hold a schwa — it's always unstressed. Just say it quickly and move on.

**Examples:** sofa, about, police, pencil, banana, family, chocolate, doctor

---

## **III. Back Vowels**

---

### **/uː/** — "oo—" (as in _food_)

**Say it like:** Pout your lips forward as if about to blow out a candle, then say "oo".
**Feel it:** Lips are rounded and pushed forward. Tongue is pulled back and high. Jaw is nearly closed.

**Examples:** food, blue, shoe, goose, group, truth, noodle, improve

---

### **/ʊ/** — "uh-oo—" (as in _foot_)

**Say it like:** Start from /uː/, then relax your lips slightly — less pout, less effort.
**Feel it:** Lips lightly rounded but not pushed forward. Tongue back but not as tense as /uː/.

> **Note:** /uː/ is long and tight; /ʊ/ is short and loose. The difference is tension, not just length.

**Examples:** put, good, look, bush, wolf, sugar, cookie, cushion

---

### **/ɔː/** — "aw—" (as in _law_)

**Say it like:** Open your mouth in a mid-sized "o" and let the sound settle back in your throat. Think of a slow "awww…" of sympathy.
**Feel it:** Lips form a loose, rounded circle. Jaw drops. Tongue sits mid-back.

**Examples:** door, thought, caught, north, war, author, pause, four

---

### **/ɒ/** — "o—" (as in _lot_)

**Say it like:** Drop your jaw and say a quick "o" — like a doctor asking you to open wide, but with the slightest lip rounding.
**Feel it:** Jaw open, lips very slightly rounded, tongue low and back.

**Examples:** dog, hot, cloth, want, honest, pocket, doctor, problem

---

### **/ɑː/** — "ah—" (as in _father_)

**Say it like:** Open your mouth as wide as is comfortable and say "ahh" — like a doctor checking your throat.
**Feel it:** Jaw fully open. Tongue flat at the bottom, pulled back slightly. No lip rounding at all.

**Examples:** car, start, heart, calm, bath, large, rather, market

# **2. NASAL & FRICATIVE SOUNDS**

---

## **I. Nasals**

> **Tip for all nasals:** The sound comes out through your *nose*, not your mouth. Pinch your nose — the sound should stop completely.

---

### **/m/** — "mmm—"

**Say it like:** Close your lips and hum. Think of "mmm" when food tastes good.
**Feel it:** Put a finger gently on your lips — you'll feel the vibration there. The sound resonates in the nose and face.

**Examples:** man, mom, lamp, summer, camera, milk, moment, hammer

---

### **/n/** — "nnn—"

**Say it like:** Close your lips as if saying "m", but then lift the tip of your tongue to touch just behind your top teeth — and hum from there. Lips stay slightly open.
**Feel it:** Touch the bridge of your nose — you'll feel a buzz. The tongue does the blocking, not the lips.

**Examples:** now, sun, rain, funny, dinner, nine, number, night

---

### **/ŋ/** — "ng—"

**Say it like:** Say "sing" and hold the very last sound — don't close your lips. The *back* of your tongue lifts to press against the roof of your mouth.
**Feel it:** The vibration moves to the back of your nose and upper throat. Lips and teeth stay open. You cannot hold this sound if your lips are closed.

**Examples:** sing, ring, long, thanks, bringing, hanging, stronger, king

---

## **II. Fricatives**

> **Tip for voiced/unvoiced pairs:** Place your hand lightly on your throat. Unvoiced sounds = no buzz. Voiced sounds = clear buzz.

---

### **/f/** — "fff—" (unvoiced)

**Say it like:** Rest your top teeth lightly on your bottom lip and blow air through — like slowly deflating a tyre.
**Feel it:** No buzz in the throat. Just a stream of air.

**Examples:** fire, life, off, photo, leaf, father, coffee, fifth

---

### **/v/** — "vvv—" (voiced)

**Say it like:** Same position as /f/ — top teeth on bottom lip — but now turn your voice on.
**Feel it:** Hand on throat — you should feel a clear buzz. If /f/ is silent wind, /v/ is buzzing wind.

**Examples:** van, cave, over, view, move, river, seven, village

---

### **/θ/** — "th—" (unvoiced, as in _thin_)

**Say it like:** Gently poke the tip of your tongue between your teeth and blow air around it. Like a very soft lisp.
**Feel it:** No vibration in the throat. Just air escaping around the tongue tip.

**Examples:** thin, path, bath, myth, cloth, think, birthday, author

---

### **/ð/** — "th—" (voiced, as in _this_)

**Say it like:** Same tongue position as /θ/ — tip between the teeth — but now add your voice.
**Feel it:** Hand on throat — buzz should be present. This "th" vibrates; the one in _thin_ does not.

**Examples:** this, then, weather, breathe, smooth, mother, clothing, father

---

### **/s/** — "sss—"

**Say it like:** A long, steady hiss — like a snake or air escaping from a tyre.
**Feel it:** No throat vibration. Tongue sides are raised, air flows through a narrow channel in the centre.

**Examples:** sun, face, glass, city, miss, sister, simple, ceiling

---

### **/z/** — "zzz—"

**Say it like:** Same as /s/, but turn your voice on — like a buzzing bee.
**Feel it:** Hand on throat — the buzz should be very clear and strong.

**Examples:** zoo, rose, bags, zero, hazy, easy, puzzle, lazy

---

### **/ʃ/** — "sh—"

**Say it like:** The sound you make to shush someone — "shhhh". Lips slightly rounded and pushed forward.
**Feel it:** The flat part of your tongue (just behind the tip) rises toward the roof of the mouth. The airflow is broader and quieter than /s/.

**Examples:** she, crash, station, chef, fish, sure, ocean, shopping

---

### **/ʒ/** — "zh—"

**Say it like:** Same mouth shape as /ʃ/, but add your voice. It's the sound in the middle of _measure_ or _vision_.
**Feel it:** Hand on throat — vibration should be present. Like a voiced, buzzing "shh".

**Examples:** measure, vision, beige, pleasure, treasure, genre, massage, explosion

# **4. DIPHTHONGS (Gliding Vowels)**

> **How to practise diphthongs:**
> 1. Say the *first* vowel and hold it for 2 seconds.
> 2. Say the *second* vowel and hold it for 2 seconds.
> 3. Then glide between them slowly in one breath.
> The movement should be smooth — like sliding, not jumping.

---

### **/aɪ/** — (as in _price_)

**Glide:** Start with a wide open "ah" (as in _father_) → slide your tongue up and forward to "ee".
**Try slowly:** "ah… ee… ah-ee… aɪ"

**Examples:** price, high, sky, night, my, time, fine, quiet

---

### **/eɪ/** — (as in _day_)

**Glide:** Start with a relaxed "eh" (as in _bed_) → glide up lightly toward "ee". The "ee" ending is subtle — don't over-pronounce it.
**Try slowly:** "eh… ee… eh-ee… eɪ"

**Examples:** day, rain, break, face, eight, name, sale, favour

---

### **/ɔɪ/** — (as in _boy_)

**Glide:** Start with a rounded "aw" (as in _law_) → glide toward "ee". Your lips start rounded and end spread.
**Try slowly:** "aw… ee… aw-ee… ɔɪ"

**Examples:** boy, choice, noise, coin, enjoy, boisterous, joining, exploit

---

### **/aʊ/** — (as in _now_)

**Glide:** Start with a wide open "ah" → round your lips and move toward "oo".
**Try slowly:** "ah… oo… ah-oo… aʊ"

**Examples:** now, town, cloud, loud, mouth, about, house, mountain

---

### **/əʊ/** — (as in _goat_)

**Glide:** Start with a lazy, unstressed "uh" (schwa) → round your lips and move toward "oo". The start is very light.
**Try slowly:** "uh… oo… uh-oo… əʊ"

**Examples:** goat, show, cold, boat, road, open, over, shoulder

---

### **/ɪə/** — (as in _near_)

**Glide:** Start with a relaxed "ih" (as in _bit_) → let everything relax and drop toward "uh". Like your voice trailing off gently.
**Try slowly:** "ih… uh… ih-uh… ɪə"

**Examples:** near, here, beer, fear, pier, weird, idea, series

---

### **/eə/** — (as in _fair_)

**Glide:** Start with "eh" (as in _bed_) → relax your tongue back and down toward "uh".
**Try slowly:** "eh… uh… eh-uh… eə"

**Examples:** fair, square, hair, there, bear, careful, wearing, spare
