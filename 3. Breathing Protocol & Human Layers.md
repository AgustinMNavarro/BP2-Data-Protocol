# **Breathing Protocol & Human Layers**

### _For Speech, Paralinguistic Textures, and Natural Vocal Behaviour_

---

This section captures the breathing sounds and small vocal noises that happen naturally in human speech — before sentences, between words, and during pauses. Just be yourself. There are no wrong sounds here.

> **General tip:** Read each description, try the sound once to get the feel of it, then record it. Don't overthink it — natural is the goal.

---

# **I. Ingressive Sounds (Breathing In)**

These are sounds made while *inhaling* — common in English conversation when agreeing, reacting, or showing attention.

---

### **1. The Inhaled "Yeah"**

_When it happens naturally:_ When someone says something and you want to agree quickly without interrupting them.

**Do this:** Say a quiet "yeah" while breathing *in* instead of out. The airflow goes inward. It should sound like a soft, breathy "yeh-" on an inhale.

- Keep lips slightly parted
- Tongue in a relaxed "y" position
- Do it 2–3 times naturally, as if in conversation

---

### **2. The Inhaled "Oh!"**

_When it happens naturally:_ A small gasp of surprise — like when you suddenly remember something you forgot.

**Do this:** Open your lips into a small "o" shape, drop your jaw slightly, and take a sharp, quick breath in. It should sound like a tiny gasp.

- Keep it short and light — not a dramatic gasp
- Do it 2–3 times

---

### **3. The Inhaled "Mm-hm"**

_When it happens naturally:_ Nodding silently to show you're listening — without interrupting the other person.

**Do this:** Close your lips, relax your tongue, and breathe in through your nose while making a soft "mm-hm" hum. The sound resonates through your nose.

- The airflow goes *inward* through the nose
- Keep it gentle and quiet
- Do it 2–3 times

---

# **II. Controlled Breaths**

These are deliberate, shaped breaths used in natural speech. Each one has a clear feeling to aim for.

---

### **1. Deep Diaphragmatic Breath**

_When it happens naturally:_ Before a long speech, when calming down, or taking a moment to reset.

**Do this:**
1. Breathe in slowly through your nose for **4 seconds** — feel your belly push out (not your chest)
2. Breathe out slowly for **6 seconds** as a steady "sssss—" or soft "hhhh—"

Repeat twice. The exhale should be long, controlled, and audible.

---

### **2. The Pre-Sentence Breath**

_When it happens naturally:_ The small intake of breath just before you start telling a story or a long sentence.

**Do this:** Take a quick, audible breath through your mouth as if you're about to say:

> "So, yesterday when I was walking to the shops…"

- The intake is short and slightly audible
- Your lips naturally form the shape of the first word coming
- Do it 3 times, each time as if you're about to launch into something

---

### **3. The Tired Sigh / Emotional Release**

_When it happens naturally:_ Sitting down after a long day. Getting news that ends your worry. Finishing something difficult.

**Do this:**
1. Take a full, slow breath in
2. Let it out as a slow, voiced "haaaaah—" — let your voice carry through the exhale
3. The pitch should be mid-to-low, relaxed, trailing off gently

Repeat 2–3 times. Each one can have a slightly different feeling — tired, relieved, or content.

---

# **III. Texture & Effort Breaths**

These capture the subtle sounds that accompany physical effort, stress, or strong emotion.

---

### **1. Light Panting**

_When it happens naturally:_ After mild exercise — climbing stairs, walking quickly, carrying something.

**Do this:** Take 5 short, quick breaths through your mouth. Mouth open, let your chest move. Don't force it — just breathe quickly and shallowly like you've been moving.

- Each breath in and out is about 1 second
- Keep it light — not dramatic, heavy panting, just mildly out of breath

---

### **2. Friction Breaths (Airy / Textured)**

_When it happens naturally:_ Whispering urgently, venting quietly, exhaling in disbelief.

**Do this:** Push air out through a slightly tightened throat or mouth — no voice, just shaped air:

- **"Hhhh—"** — open throat, like fogging up a mirror
- **"Sss—"** — narrow the air through your teeth like a slow hiss

Do each 2–3 times. The goal is audible air *with texture*, not clean silence.

---

### **3. Glottal Catch (Throat Catch)**

_When it happens naturally:_ Trying not to cry. Hiccupping gently. A "hard start" before an emotional word.

**Do this:**
1. Take a quick breath in
2. Make 3–4 short "uh-uh-uh" sounds by stopping and releasing air at the back of your throat — like tiny hiccups

- The catch happens in the throat, not the lips
- Keep it gentle — not a choking sound, just a tight, soft flutter

---

### **4. Frustration / Effort Bursts**

_When it happens naturally:_ Struggling with something stuck, reacting to mild annoyance, physical effort.

**Do this:** Push short, sharp bursts of air. Say each one 2–3 times:

- **"Tch—"** — tongue clicks to the roof of the mouth, then releases air (like "tsk")
- **"Hf—"** — a quick push from the belly, lips barely open (like a small frustrated huff)
- **"Kh—"** — a rough push from the back of the throat (like a very quiet throat-clear)

Keep them natural — imagine something mildly frustrating just happened.

---

# **IV. Natural Silence & Ambient Sounds**

These capture the small, human sounds that happen when you're *not* speaking. They are just as important as the speech itself.

---

### **1. Natural Pause (10 seconds)**

_What it captures:_ The way a real person sounds between sentences — breathing, small shifts, the room.

**Do this:** Sit still and breathe normally for 10 seconds. Don't try to be silent or perfectly still. Just be natural. Small sounds are fine — a quiet nose breath, a slight throat movement, a body shift.

> Do not hold your breath. The goal is natural human presence, not dead silence.

---

### **2. Idle Mouth Sounds**

_What it captures:_ The tiny sounds a mouth makes between words — present in all close-microphone recordings.

**Do this:** Relax completely and let these happen naturally:

- A small lip part or click
- A quiet swallow
- Tongue repositioning

Don't force them. Just open your mouth slightly, relax, and let whatever happens, happen. Record for about 10 seconds.

---

### **3. Soft Throat Movement**

_What it captures:_ The subtle swallows and micro-adjustments the throat makes during natural speech pauses.

**Do this:** Swallow gently, or make a very small throat-settling movement — not a cough or a full throat-clear, just the tiny shift your throat makes naturally when you're relaxed.

Do this 2–3 times, spaced a few seconds apart.
