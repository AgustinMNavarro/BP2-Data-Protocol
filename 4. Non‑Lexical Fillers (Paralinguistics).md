# **Non‑Lexical Fillers (Paralinguistics)**

### _English-Language Version_

Non‑lexical fillers train the model to sound **human, expressive, emotional, and spontaneous** rather than robotic or text-to-speech–like.  
These sounds capture the **reactive, emotional, conversational micro‑moments** that English speakers use constantly.

All fillers should be recorded:

- **3–5 repetitions each**
- With **pitch variation** (flat, rising, falling)
- At **three intensity levels**:
    - **50% volume** (quiet, subtle)
    - **80% volume** (normal, conversational)
    - **100% volume** (fully expressive, emotional)

---

# **I. Core English Non‑Lexical Fillers (Cultural Essentials)**

These are the **core emotional sounds** English speakers use for agreement, hesitation, surprise, emotional reaction, and conversational rhythm.

---

### **1. “Oh” — The Swiss-Army Knife of English**

Used for reaction, soft surprise, sympathy, understanding, disappointment, or realisation.

Variants to record:

- **Short/Flat:** “Oh.” (neutral acknowledgment)
- **Rising:** “Oh?” (surprise / curiosity)
- **Falling/Soft:** “Oohh…” (sympathy/realisation)
- **Long/Deep:** “Oooohhh—” (big reveal, impressed)

This is one of the most important English filler sounds.

---

### **2. “Hmm” — Thinking / Processing**

- Mouth closed, nasal resonance
- For skepticism, thoughtfulness, hesitation

Variants:

- Short: “Hm.”
- Long: “Hmmm—”
- Rising: “Hmm?”
- Falling: “Hmmm…”

---

### **3. “Uh‑huh” — Affirmation / Agreement**

A universal backchannel in English conversations.

Variants:

- Flat: “Uh‑huh.”
- Rising (encouraging): “Uh‑HUH?”
- Falling (agreement): “Uh‑huh…”

Record both **voiced** and **more nasal** variants.

---

### **4. “Mm‑hm” — Softer Agreement**

Mouth closed, nasal.

Variants:

- “Mm‑hm.”
- “Mmm‑HMM?”
- “Mm‑hm…”

---

### **5. “Huh?” — Confusion / Not Hearing**

Variants:

- Sharp confusion: “Huh?”
- Softer: “Huhh…”
- Falling: “Huh.” (resigned, puzzled)

---

### **6. “Uh / Um” — Hesitation**

Two of the most essential English hesitation markers.

Variants:

- Short hesitation: “Uh…”
- Longer filler: “Ummm…”
- Rising “Um?”
- Nervous “Uh‑uh‑uh” series

---

### **7. “Wow” — Impression / Emotion**

Record:

- Short: “Wow.”
- Whispered: “wow…”
- Rising excitement: “Wow!”
- Long: “Wooooow—”

---

### **8. “Yikes / Eek / Oof” — Small Exclamation Set**

- “Yikes!” (shock)
- “Eek!” (surprise)
- “Oof.” (impact / empathy)

---

# **II. Reactionary & Breath‑Based Fillers**

Record all at 50%, 80%, and 100% volume.

---

### **1. “Aha” — Understanding / Discovery**

Focus on the friction in the middle: “ha.”

Variants:

- Bright rising: “A‑HA!”
- Soft realisation: “Ah‑ha…”
- Short: “Aha.”

---

### **2. Small Laughs**

- **Short laugh:** “Ha!”
- **Soft chuckle:** “heh-heh”
- **Breathy laugh:** “hahaha—” (fading out)

---

### **3. Breath Reactions**

- **Gasp / Inhale:** “_gasp_”
- **Small inhale:** (quick rising intake)
- **Tired breath:** “Hhh—”

---

### **4. Disgust / Distaste Sounds**

Closest English equivalents to Danish _Øv_ or “Ad”.

Record:

- “Ugh.”
- “Ew.”
- “Bleh.” (mild disgust)
- Long displeasure: “Uggghhh—”

---

### **5. Clicks / Attention Sounds**

- **Tsk‑tsk** (disapproval)
- **Psst** (getting attention)
- **Lip smack** (beginning of speech)

---

# **IV. Updated RVC Recording Sequence for English**

Record each **cluster** 3 times (normal, rising, falling intonation).

---

### **1. The “Thinker” Cluster**

Sequence (repeat 3 times):

1. Deep nasal inhale
2. “Hmmmm—”
3. “Aha!”

---

### **2. The “Clumsy” Cluster**

Sequence:

1. Sharp intake
2. “Oops!”
3. “Ugh…” (falling pitch)

---

### **3. The “Agreeable” Cluster**

Sequence:

1. Small inhale / gasped agreement
2. “Mm‑hm.” (rising)
3. “Ohh.” (understanding)

---

### **4. The “Physical” Cluster**

Sequence:

1. Light cough
2. “Oof.”
3. “Psst.”
4. “Tsk‑tsk.”
